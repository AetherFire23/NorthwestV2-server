**Meta**

**Servers (Matchmaking Basis)**

- **Every game takes place on a server.**
- **Each server has two fixed settings:**
  - **Time Zone → ensures players are grouped by compatible play hours.**
  - **Language → ensures players can communicate smoothly.**

**Game Levels**

- **Each game also has a level rating: ★ to ★★★★★.**
- **Levels reflect access restrictions based on honor and talent:**
- ★ **Minimal** → **Open Deck**
- ★★ **Superior Honor** → **Trusted Crew**
- ★★★ **Superior Talent and Honor** → **Proven Voyage**
- ★★★★ **Optimal Honor + Superior Talent** → **Exemplary Service**
- ★★★★★ **Optimal Honor and Talent** → **Admiralty Court**

**Ticks System**

- **Each in-game day is divided into 4 phases (ticks):**
  - **09:00 → 12:00**
  - **12:00 → 15:00**
  - **15:00 → 18:00**
  - **18:00 → 21:00**
- **At the start of each tick:**
  - **Players receive TP (time points).**
  - **All tick-relevant mechanics resolve (production, sabotage traces, sanity/health changes, etc.).**
- **TP accumulation:**
  - **Players can carry unused TP across ticks, up to a limit.**
  - **Cap varies by game level:**
    - **★ Open Deck → 8 ticks (~2 days).**
    - **★★ Trusted Crew → 6 ticks.**
    - **★★★ Proven Voyage → 6 ticks (~1.5 days).**
    - **★★★★ Exemplary Service → 4 ticks.**
    - **★★★★★ Admiralty Court → 4 ticks (~1 day).**
- **Inquiry & presence:**
  - **Entering a room at any point during a tick registers the player as being “in that room” for the whole tick.**
  - **This avoids timing disputes and ensures sabotage suspicion applies to all present equally.**

**End-game scorings**

**Talent System**

- **Outcome-based:** After each game, every player either *wins* or *loses*.
- **Effect:**
  - Victory → talent increases.
  - Defeat → talent decreases.
- **Teamwide:** All members of the same side gain or lose together, regardless of survival.
- **Difficulty adjustment:** Calculated from **average loyal talent vs. average cultist talent**.
  - Higher difficulty → bigger reward for victory, softer penalty for defeat.
- **Meta-balance:** Gain rates for loyals and cultists auto-adjust over time to preserve fairness in the meta.
- **Non-zero sum:** A game *creates* talent; gains are not offset by others’ losses.
- **Threshold of meaning:** Talent only counts after ~12 games (no score before that).
- **Reward:** Grants access to **exclusive games**.

**Honor System**

- **Feedback after each game:**
  - Players may **praise** or **blame** others, but they are not expected to vote on everyone.
  - If they have no opinion, they should give neither praise nor blame.
- **Criteria:**
  - **Praise** → for players who were active, pleasant, and showed sportsmanship.
  - **Blame** → for players who were inactive, disruptive, or unpleasant.
  - *Being a cultist is never grounds for blame in itself.*
- **Weighting of Opinions:**
  - The **honor level of the giver** scales the value of their vote.
  - High-honor players’ judgments weigh more.
  - Low-honor players’ judgments weigh less.
- **Consensus Enforcement:**
  - Praise and blame are designed to reflect the **majority spirit**, not raw vote counts.
  - Outlier votes against the consensus are discounted and penalized.
- **Special Rule: Cultists**
  - **Praise from another cultist** → does not count.
  - **Blame from another cultist** → counts heavily, since it may mean sabotage of their own team.
  - **Loyal judgments against cultists** are expected to be biased.
    - Loyal *blame* for cultists is only valid if it is **strongly consensual**.
    - If even a few loyals praise a cultist, then scattered loyal blames are considered **dissident** (and punished as such), *even if they are the majority*.
    - The principle: *a cultist who behaved pleasantly should not be punished in honor just for being a cultist*.
- **Rewards:**
  - Honor grants access to **exclusive games**.
  - Among Chaplain volunteers, the one with the **highest honor** becomes Chaplain.

**Captain: Privilege by wealth**

- Entry comes from resources (auction) rather than reputation.
- Elite inquiry power, but *not guaranteed loyal*. That means his words carry enormous weight but can also be deliberate manipulation.
- Mechanically reinforced with *command* (collective efficiency), *congratulation* (sanity), and control of the *jail key* and initial armory access.
- Stronger combat loadout (sword + pistol) makes him much harder to assassinate than the Chaplain. He is not defenseless — indeed, a Captain cultist is one of the scariest roles in the game.
- His authority is structural, not earned. It reflects both the Royal Navy’s aristocratic order and the tension you’ve baked into the design: privilege does not mean virtue.

**Chaplain: Privilege by trust**

- Entry comes only from accumulated honor, based on post-game peer judgment.
- Elite inquiry power, but *always loyal*. That makes him valuable but also fragile — a cultist has every reason to strike him down early.
- Weak in combat, reliant on allies, and socially vulnerable if unpopular.
- His authority is fragile, constantly tested, and must be *maintained* across sessions.

**Effects on experience and money**

**Experience**

- **No gameplay edge** — experienced players and new players compete on equal footing.
- **Access to exclusive games** → pools players with comparable experience for higher-level social play.
- **Exclusive skins** → cosmetic rewards tied to accumulated playtime/track record.

**Money**

- **No gameplay edge** — paying players and non-paying players remain equal in mechanics.
- **Exclusive skins** → cosmetic variety purchasable with money.
- **Captain auctions** → money allows bidding for the Captain role (structural privilege, not mechanical strength).

**Combined (Experience + Money)**

- **Champion skins** → a small set of rare cosmetics available only to players who both invest time and contribute money.

**Skins & Social Effects**

- Skins are purely **cosmetic**; they reveal a player’s **experience** and/or **money**.
- No in-game mechanical effect — but they can shape **social perception** (prestige, suspicion, intimidation, or ridicule).

**Veteran & Expert Roles (concept for later development)**

- **Acquisition**
  - Each game played in a role grants **role-specific experience**.
  - **Victory** gives more experience, but **defeat still gives some** (unlike talent).
  - Unlock thresholds are deliberately high:
    - Dozens of games → **Veteran** version.
    - 100+ games → **Expert** version.
- **Nature of Veteran/Expert Roles**
  - Not mechanically “stronger” in a straightforward way.
  - They introduce **both bonuses and flaws**:
    - More tools, but also more liabilities.
    - More potential power, but greater risk of backfiring.
  - Intended for seasoned players who know how to manage the pitfalls.
- **Design Principle**
  - **Inexperienced players are not disadvantaged.**
    - Basic roles remain balanced and competitive.
    - Veteran/Expert variants are *swingier* — in the wrong hands, they can be worse than the basic version.
  - In practice: only players with real experience will consistently benefit from them.
- **Rare downside**
  - The only case where “lack of access” is a genuine disadvantage is if an inexperienced player *would* have used the advanced role well *and* avoided its risks — but this is deliberately rare.
