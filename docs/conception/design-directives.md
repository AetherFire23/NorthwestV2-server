**Design directives for Northwest**

**Design Directive: Social Deduction First, Simulation Second**

**Core mandate:**
Every mechanic in *Northwest* must serve the social deduction loop. Simulation detail is permitted only insofar as it creates structured dilemmas, suspicion, and debate. The system does not aim to replicate an expedition realistically, but to generate meaningful *talking points* at the table.

**Principles**

- **Discussion Over Calculation**
  - Outcomes must be *legible* to players in social terms (“Why were you in that room?”) rather than buried in math.
  - Granular tracking (e.g. sickness scores) should resolve into *discrete states* that matter for trust and survival debates.
- **Ambiguity With Teeth**
  - Every signal is interpretable both ways: loyal or acolyte.
  - No result should ever be absolute, but each should push players toward an argument they *must* make.
- **Symbolic Weight Over Realism**
  - Items, statuses, and crises exist as *symbols of loyalty and betrayal*.
  - A sword is not just combat strength — it’s who dares carry it, and why. A fire is not just damage — it’s a test of blame and alibi.
- **Event as Prompt**
  - The purpose of a mechanic is to *generate a prompt for social play*.
  - Example: a random fire matters not for the damage itself, but because it creates a suspect pool, forces collaboration, and sparks accusations.
- **Clarity of Stakes**
  - Simulation detail is only justified when it clarifies *what players risk by acting or not acting*.
  - If a mechanic adds realism without shaping debate or tension, it must be abstracted.
- **Narrative Rhythm**
  - The pacing of mechanics (ticks, crises, reveals) must ensure continuous *cycles of discussion*.
  - Each phase should leave the crew with something to argue about, not just something to compute.

**Design Aim**

Northwest is not a survival simulator. It is a **deduction engine dressed as survival**: a game where food, fire, sickness, and violence all matter less for their simulationist detail than for the suspicion they generate. Every mechanic must therefore be judged by one question: *does it make the conversation richer, sharper, more dramatic?*

**Design Directive: Balanced Inquiry Principle**

Northwest’s social deduction must always arise from a **fusion** of two equally weighted inputs:

- **Mechanical Inquiry** → noisy, probabilistic pings and traces from sabotage, inspections, presence windows, and role abilities.
- **Survival Reads** → interpretation of survival-related choices (resource use, expeditions, repairs, combat, medicine, leadership).

Core mandate:

- **No action or ping is ever absolute**. Every piece of information is *arguable*, but also *anchored in mechanics* so that debates do not collapse into pure gut reads.
- **Every accusation must mix both streams.** Purely mechanical deductions are always noisy; purely behavioral reads are always explainable. Strength comes from how they overlap.

Key principles:

- **Variable reliability**: Mechanical inquiry ranges from low to high credibility, but never beyond the 95% certainty ceiling.
- **Interpretive survival play**: Every survival choice can be defended as loyal or attacked as cultist, depending on timing, efficiency, and alternatives.
- **Accusation ecology**: Strong accusations emerge when *mechanical pings* and *survival reads* converge, but the floor of doubt (≥5%) always leaves room for counterplay.
- **Dynamic balance**: Survival efficiency and inquiry clarity are in tension. Over-focus on survival blurs inquiry; over-focus on inquiry risks mission failure.

Design aim:
This principle ensures that Northwest avoids the two failures of most social deduction games:

- **Psychology-only** games (Werewolf, Town of Salem) where accusations rest on tone or gut feeling alone.
- **Binary-information** games (classic Mafia roles, hard “seers”) where accusations reduce to “believe me or believe him.”

Northwest instead creates **living, arguable deductions**: a perpetual middle ground where debates are mechanically grounded yet never certain, ensuring that both loyal and cultist play remains rich, flexible, and dramatic.

**Design Directive: Random Events and Deterministic Player Actions**

**Determinism**

- All player actions — production, combat, acolyte actions — have deterministic outcomes.
- Bad luck is never an excuse for poor results.
- The **sole exception** is inquiry, which always remains probabilistic and never fully certain.

**Random Events as the Survival Baseline**

- Every game contains a fixed total **severity** of random events (storms, fires, accidents, nightmares, hallucinations, etc.).
- This guarantees equal survival pressure across all voyages.
- The **spread per phase** is preset but varies from game to game:
  - Prevents long “empty” phases with no pressure.
  - Prevents meta-deduction (players can’t know if *this event* was baseline or acolyte).
- Composition is randomized: one game may feature many small fires, another a few devastating storms.

**Acolyte Actions Within Crises**

Acolytes interact with crises in **two ways**:

- **Mimicked crises** (ambiguous)
  - Some acolyte actions reproduce the exact effects of random events (e.g. causing a fire, worsening a storm, triggering a hallucination).
  - In these cases, the crew can never be sure if the crisis was random or fueled by acolytes.
- **Distinct crises** (certainly acolyte-caused)
  - Other acolyte actions create crises that no random event can produce (e.g. a curse, a demonic scream, a supernatural infestation).
  - These are always known to be acolyte actions, though the suspect pool remains uncertain.

**Balance Between Mimicked and Distinct Crises**

- Mimicked crises are inherently more powerful because they blend into randomness:
  - They provide a built-in alibi: “That could just have been random.”
  - Even if an acolyte is the **only player** in the suspect pool, they may still claim innocence credibly — since random events can occur without any player present at all.
- **To remain balanced**, mimicked acolyte actions must:
  - Carry a **higher rogue/occult cost**, and/or
  - Require **more exclusive conditions** (fewer players eligible, thus smaller suspect pools).

**Design Directive: Player Choice Significance & Crew Tension**

- **Scarcity of TP**
- There are always more urgent needs than available time.
- Every TP spent has an opportunity cost — pursuing one path means neglecting another.
- **The Two Planes of Play**
- **Operations**: keeping the expedition alive (food, repairs, morale, survival tasks).
- **Inquiry**: uncovering loyalties (rogue traces, suspect blocks, scans, debates).
- Both are indispensable: operations without inquiry leaves the cultists unchecked; inquiry without operations leaves the crew to attrition.
- **Interdependence of Planes**
- The design does not force an absolute choice between the two; instead, most meaningful actions *touch both planes at once*.
  - Example: preparing food is operationally vital, but who joins the kitchen and when may become part of a suspect block.
  - Example: scanning a suspect informs inquiry, but costs TP that could have prevented real shortages.
- Thus, even “operational” choices carry inquiry weight, and vice versa.
- **Significance Through Tension**
- Players must constantly justify their TP allocation.
- The debate is never abstract: each TP spent is felt both in survival outcomes and in the inquiry narrative.
- The design ensures that the most contested decisions are those that **matter doubly** — pressing to survival *and* exposing or obscuring loyalties.
- **Dynamic Balance**
- There is no stable equilibrium: leaning into one plane too heavily erodes the other.
- The survival of the crew depends on continual recalibration, where each round’s allocations redefine the balance of power.
- This constant tension guarantees that choices are always consequential and politically charged.

**Design Directive: The Late-Game Reveal Calculus**

Core mandate:
By the late game, cultists face a genuine, agonizing decision: continue the mask of loyalty, or drop it for direct aggression.

Dynamics:

- **Mask Value (MV)**: Strategic worth of maintaining trust (access, influence, subtle sabotage).
- **Reveal Value (RV)**: Tactical punch of going loud (sudden sabotage, open strikes, seizing control).

Arc:

- **Early rounds**: MV ≫ RV — masks are precious; any reveal is ruinous.
- **Midgame**: MV declines (suspicion builds), RV rises (gameboard swingy, small hits matter).
- **Endgame**: MV and RV are close; the right decision depends on true vs. perceived trust.

Design aim:
The late-game reveal is never automatically correct. Overestimating or underestimating one’s trust level leads to bad reveals or missed opportunities. This forces traitors into high-stakes judgment calls, while the crew braces for both stealth and sudden violence.

**Design Directive: Trust Categories as Specialisations**

**Core mandate:**
Every role in *Northwest* can engage in production, inquiry, and combat. The three categories — **Producers, Inquirers, Fighters** — are not restrictions but **specialisations**: they define where a role exerts greater leverage and where their actions carry heavier social weight.

**Dynamics:**

- **Universal capability:** All players are expected to contribute to all three fields. A Brute may cook, a Scholar may fight, a Cook may inquire.
- **Leverage, not dominance:** A category advantage never ensures superiority. Three non-Fighters can overpower a Fighter; several non-Producers can outproduce a Producer. The specialist’s edge is in efficiency and authority, not absolute outcomes.
- **Dual pressures:** Specialists are judged both for raw results and for who benefits from those results. Producing for allies, clearing or ignoring leads, or striking at one target instead of another is as socially important as the act itself.
- **Interdependence:** Because all roles can do everything, no specialist stands alone. Specialists must rely on others to supplement their weaknesses, making cooperation and suspicion inseparable.

**Design aim:**
The categories exist to **shape the player’s experience**. Producers live under pressure to distribute or withhold resources fairly. Inquirers bear the weight of credibility and persuasion. Fighters navigate the risk of decisive violence. Together they form distinct but overlapping playstyles that align with *Northwest*’s design goal of **variable player experiences**: some roles are constant, high-pressure (Captain, Chaplain), others are situational spikes (Marine, Sapper), and others are steady support with bursts of centrality (Cook, Doctor). This variety makes each game feel different and lets players choose roles that match their desired intensity, ensuring replayability and diverse social dynamics.

**Design Directive: Inquiry, Gauges & Acolyte Actions**

**1. Time–Space Traces (the inquiry substrate)**

- **Presence windows**: every acolyte action keys off being in a room during the activation tick. All who satisfy that window are logged as suspects.
- **Room registers**: anyone who enters a room during a tick is considered “in that room” for the whole tick — no erasure is possible.
- **Relative justification**: all players in a suspect block must defend why they were there; weak or opportunistic excuses are thinner defenses.
- **Critical risk**: triggering an acolyte action while alone in its window would expose the actor with certainty — a complete blunder.

**2. Social Geometry of Suspect Blocks**

- Acolytes among trusted players risk exposure; among weaker justifications, they can diffuse suspicion.
- Both loyals and acolytes may use reverse psychology (“If I were an acolyte, why would I expose myself here?”).
- The **weight of trust** within a suspect block may matter more than the raw number of suspects.

**3. TP & Bookkeeping Rules**

- TP is public and fully compiled by the system; no advantage exists in manual tracking.
- Acolyte actions **cost no TP**. Their price is entirely in gauge movement (rogue/occult) and the suspect blocks they generate.
- No fake TP entries are ever created. Bookkeeping must remain clean, with no reverse-engineerable gaps.

**4. Movement & Grouping Tension**

- **Inquiry pushes to spread**: smaller groups sharpen suspicion.
- **Survival pushes to gather**: larger groups improve efficiency and safety.
- **The rogue gauge**

**Rogue gauge (per player)**

- **Visible rogue gauge** (all players):
  - Starts at **20% baseline**.
  - Fills only through **loyal rogue actions** (cover-seeking actions that appear suspicious but are not treason).
  - Decays naturally back toward 20%.
  - Determines the chance of rogue pings for loyal players.
- **Secret rogue gauge** (acolytes only):
  - Filled exclusively by **acolyte actions**.
  - Independent from the visible gauge.
  - Can exceed **100%**, allowing rogue scans to reach certain detection even with limited accuracy.
  - Decays naturally over time back to 0%.

🔑 **Design Aim:**
This directive ensures every acolyte action creates inquiry opportunities. Presence windows, gauges, and scans weave together into a noisy but grounded information fabric, where nothing is ever certain but everything is arguable.

**Design Directive: Inquiry Tools and Reliability**

**Inquiry in Northwest relies on scans.** Each scan produces a binary ping — suspicious or not suspicious. These pings are never absolute, but their patterns shape deduction and debate.

**Two types of scans**

- **Rogue scans (low-alarm):**
  - Probe the rogue gauge of a player.
  - For loyals, this means their **visible rogue gauge**, which always starts at 20%, rises only through loyal rogue actions, and decays back toward the baseline.
  - For acolytes, this means their **secret rogue gauge**, which rises only through acolyte actions, can exceed 100%, and also decays naturally.
  - A suspicious ping is never a fabrication. It always means the relevant rogue gauge is above zero. Statistically speaking, there are no false negatives. Logically, the false negatives correspond to the 20% visible rogue gauge baseline.
  - Acolytes with secret rogue at 100% will always ping positive.
  - Loyals remain arguable because their baseline 20% can trigger suspicion even when innocent.
- **Identity scans (high-alarm):**
  - Probe loyalty directly.
  - Their uncertainty comes from both **false positives** and **false negatives**, distributed asymmetrically with more false positives.
  - They are more reliable for **clearing loyals** than for condemning acolytes.
  - Every repeated identity scan on the same target has diminishing worth, and this loss cannot be refreshed.

🔑 **Design Aim:** Rogue scans provide noise that never fabricates guilt, but keeps everyone arguable. Identity scans provide clarity that never proves betrayal, but stabilizes trust. Together, they ensure inquiry is always noisy, arguable, and socially driven — never mechanical proof, never empty guesswork.

**Design Directive: Hunger, Feeding Patterns & Cook Influence**

**Core mandate:**
Every player must eat daily. Hunger, sickness, and morale together make food management the most constant survival pressure. The Cook, by controlling access to prepared meals, becomes a permanent center of influence.

**Hunger Mechanic**

- Each day without eating increases a player’s *Hunger status* by 1.
- Hunger is cumulative and persistent: missing 3 meals creates Hunger (3), which requires 3 meals to return to normal.
- Hunger handicaps scale with severity: from mild weakness to near-incapacitation. The penalty is reversible only through consistent eating.

**Rations as Baseline**

- Rations (uncooked cans) are the universal fallback. Any player can consume them instantly, without Cook involvement.
- However, rations are corrupted. Each ration increases a hidden *Ration Counter* that compounds sickness risk over time. A few rations may be tolerated, but long reliance guarantees severe illness.
- This mechanic preserves agency (no player is ever completely denied food) but ensures rations are an unsustainable strategy.

**Prepared Meals & Cook Influence**

- Prepared meals reset daily hunger without increasing sickness risk. Some also provide Hope boosts.
- Meals require TP and resources. If a non-Cook prepares them, the TP cost is tripled.
- This mechanic ensures the Cook is permanently influential: players must either secure his favor or pay dearly to circumvent him.
- Every meal creates a distribution event: *who eats, who does not, and who receives Hope boosts.* These choices draw a visible “trust map” around the Cook, shaping alliances and accusations.

**Feeding Patterns as Inquiry**

- Because hunger persists and Hope is scarce, *feeding patterns become inquiry material*.
  - A Cook who consistently favors the same few players creates suspicion of favoritism or collusion.
  - A refusal to feed a desperate crew member may be seen as prudent triage — or a cultist’s execution by neglect.
  - Heavy reliance on rations blurs inquiry, as ration sickness can be mistaken for poisoning.
- Thus, every feeding decision can be defended as loyal survival or attacked as sabotage, keeping debates permanently arguable.

**Design Aim:**
The hunger system guarantees that food is never “just survival.” It is a *political economy* in which the Cook mediates both health and trust. Rations keep players alive but sick, prepared meals restore hope but require alliances, and every pattern of inclusion or exclusion defines social perception. This ensures that the Cook’s influence is continuous, visible, and contentious, anchoring inquiry directly to the core survival loop.
