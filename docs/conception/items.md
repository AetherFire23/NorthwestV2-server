**Items**

**Item Mechanics (General)**

**1. Carrying Capacity**

- Each player has a fixed number of carry capacity: 5. (exception, the Brute can carry 8)
- Items occupy between 1–3 capacity depending on size/weight.
- Carry slots are a strategic limiter: holding powerful gear reduces ability to transport resources or tools.
- Trade-off: more combat readiness vs. more logistical contribution.

**2. Public vs. Hidden State**

- Items can be **publicly held** or **secretly dropped/given**.
- Public items are recorded as “last held by [player],” creating a traceable chain of custody.
- A player may later declare publicly that they no longer hold an item, even if the transfer/drop was hidden.
- Hidden items must be searched for unless the player who dropped them reveals the location.
- Dual-cause design: hiding items can signal sabotage or simple self-protection, ensuring permanent ambiguity.

**3. Dropping and Storing**

- Items may be dropped in rooms.
  - **High-traffic rooms**: thefts are harder to attribute (many suspects, broad inquiry).
  - **Low-traffic rooms**: fewer suspects but higher inquiry precision if something goes missing.
- Specialized storage rooms exist (e.g. Armory, Magazine).
  - Storage ensures safety but transfers control to whoever holds the key.
  - Refusing to use storage is socially charged — may be justified (quick access) or suspicious (planning concealment).

**4. Resource Items**

- Some items are resource packs.
- All resource packs count as 1 carry slot.
- Some resources are unlimited, constrained only by carrying capacity and time.
- Others are limited, creating scarcity disputes and inquiry pressure.
- Expeditions may introduce new resource packs, expanding or complicating scarcity.

**5. Tool Items**

- Tools gate specific actions (e.g. repairs, inquiry, production, combat).
- Some are common and replaceable; others are rare and high-stakes.
- Dual-use design: every tool has legitimate loyal purposes but also sabotage or deception potential.

**6. Combat Items**

- Weapons and explosives grant combat power but have strong trade-offs:
  - Large carry values reduce hauling efficiency.
  - Visible ownership creates suspicion or deterrence.
  - Concealable items allow for surprise attacks but invite paranoia.
- Environment modifiers apply: some items are stronger in confined interiors, others in open exteriors.
- Special-case rooms may shift modifiers, adding situational variety.

**7. Keys and Access Items**

- Some items provide access to rooms or storage (e.g. jail, armory, magazine).
- Keys are carryable items themselves.
- Control of keys creates choke points in trust and power:
  - A loyal keyholder ensures structure and safety.
  - A cultist keyholder weaponizes bottlenecks, thefts, or delays.

**8. Inquiry Layer of Items**

- Every item action (pick up, drop, transfer, store, use, conceal) generates inquiry opportunities.
- Losses of scarce or critical items always spark debate.
- Storage choices, declarations of loss, and refusals to use certain items serve as social signals.
- No item state can ever be interpreted with certainty: every action has at least one loyal and one traitor explanation.

**Poison logs and supply management**:

- **Food tracking (meals, cans, flour, meat, fish):**
Every food item keeps a **log of suspects**: anyone who either carried the item or was merely present in a room where it was stored. Both counts equally as potential poison opportunities.
→ Each consumable meal therefore comes with an explicit suspect list.
- **Hidden periods:**
If a food item is ever hidden, its existing log is **erased**. A new log is only created once the item is declared public again. In addition, the log will always show the mention **“hidden period”** — with no further detail — marking the gap of uncertainty.
- **Supply protection:**
Food items **in the supply cannot be poisoned**. Once picked up from the supply, they cannot be put back into it. From the moment they leave the supply, they start generating a log of suspects.

**Resources – limited supply for each**

**Cans**

- **Start:** Stocked in Food Store.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Cooking meals

**Flour**

- **Start:** Stocked in Food Store.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use: Cooking meals**

**Liquor**

- **Start:** Stocked in Food Store.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Medicine (antiseptic, morale boost) or indulgence (sanity effects, possible brawls)

**Coal**

- **Start:** Stocked in Coal Store.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Fuel for boiler for warmth.

**Gunpowder**

- **Start:** Stocked in Magazine.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Crafting of firearms and explosives, charging the cannons and the mortar.

**Scrap**

- **Start:** Stocked in Hold.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Engineering and research projects; crafting of high-impact tools (e.g., grenades).

**Weapons**

**Knife**

- **Start:** Cook and Ranger start with one.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Weak melee weapon. Stronger indoors than outdoors. Concealable.
- **Upgraded version:** Dagger

**Sword**

- **Start:** Captain and Marine start with one.
- **Carry value:** 2.
- **Use:** Strong melee weapon. Stronger outdoors than indoors. Visible, not concealable.
- **Upgraded version:** Sabre

**Pistol**

- **Start:** Captain and Sapper start with one.
- **Carry value:** 2.
- **Use:** Weak ranged weapon. Stronger indoors than outdoors. Concealable.
- **Upgraded version:** Revolver

**Musket**

- **Start:** Marine and Ranger start with one.
- **Carry value:** 3.
- **Use:** Strong ranged weapon. Stronger outdoors than indoors. Visible.
- **Upgraded version:** Rifle

**Keys**

**Prison Key**

- **Start:** Captain.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Required to jail or release prisoners.

**Armory Key**

- **Start:** Quartermaster.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Unlocks weapon storage.

**Magazine Key**

- **Start:** Quartermaster.
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Unlocks gunpowder supply.

**Tools**

**Fishing Pole**

- **Start:** One in Hold
- **Carry value:** 2.
- **Use:** Allows fishing on deck. Inefficient food source; mostly a time sink.

**Spyglass**

- **Start:** Sentry
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Enhances inquiry and survival events, especially expeditions (spotting cairns, monsters, etc.).

**Hammer**

- **Start:** Two in Hold
- **Carry value:** 2.
- **Use:** Required for all repairs.
- important but risky.

**Dynamite**

- **Start:** None
- **Carry value:** 2
- **Use:** Major boon in expeditions (clear obstacles, kill monsters). Can also cause catastrophic sabotage.

**Grenade**

- **Start:** None
- **Carry value:** 1.
- **Use:** Pure combat. Indoors: hits an entire room + fire risk. Outdoors: better targeted.

**Clothes**

**Uniform**

- **Carry value**: 2 or 0 if worn in the special clothing slot.
- **Effects**: Baseline protection against “no clothes” hope/sickness penalties.

**Dirty Uniform**

- **Carry value**: 2 or 0 if worn in the special clothing slot
- **Effects**: Baseline protection against “no clothes” hope/sickness penalties. Slowly erodes hope over time.

**Coat**

- **Carry value**: 2 or 0 if worn in the special clothing slot.
- **Effects**: Identical to uniform while one ship. Essential for survival during expeditions.

**Dirty Coat**

- **Carry value**: 2 or 0 if worn in the special clothing slot.
- **Effects**: Identical to dirty uniform while one ship. Essential for survival during expeditions. Slowly erodes hope over time.
